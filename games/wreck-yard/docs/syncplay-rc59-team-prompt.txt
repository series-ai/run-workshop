2. Investigate sustained activity after torch release.

The fresh rc.59 real-touch route passes six functional checks and ten
segments. Idle/held/final-settle display rates are 59.97/54.51/51.98 Hz.
After 60 seconds, native step plus output p95 is 22.30 ms versus 2.83 ms
idle. Median active bodies are 453 versus 2 idle. Input is neutral.
Cuts stop at 18 after release, but fractures grow from zero to 17. Native
bodies grow from 697 to 748; render bodies grow from 544 to 595.

Performance does not return to idle. The previous rc.57 route ended near
58 Hz, with 15 cuts and 4 fractures. Outcomes differ, so do not call this
an isolated solver regression. Investigate active islands, contact/wake
propagation, and the continuing fracture workload. The trace has not
isolated the cause. No functional, page, shader, or session errors occur.

3. Explain or repair the cylinder-stack settling delay.

Five loose tires are awake at frames 60, 90, and 120, then sleep by 180 and
stay asleep through 900. There are no fractures. This matches rc.57.
Official rc.56 sleeps by the frame-60 sample. The existing frame-90 props
test fails. This is a settling delay, not permanent activity.

4. Supply supported mobile history settings and measured limits.

Seat context and history options shipped in rc.57. They remain available,
not missing APIs. Wreck Yard must still consume the third step argument
and configure retention. Its unchanged four-player storage test peaks at
174.94 MiB of raw JS physics history and about 22.55 MiB native retained
bytes. These metrics overlap. Old-state replay and disposal pass.

The new checkpoint sharing and serialization do not compress or share
older exported JS byte saves. Default history remains 256. Give a tested
combination of snapshotBufferSize, maxInputRetentionFrames, checksumCadenceTicks,
maxSnapshotBytes, and minimumSnapshotWitnesses. Validate rollback, donation,
late join, long-session retention, and replay-export limits together.
Measure a phone. These desktop results are not a phone budget or leak claim.

5. Validate bounded recovery and missing-target donor handling.

The rc.57 scheduling, diagnostics, exact-target donor refusal, and rotation
repairs remain. The confirmation loop has no per-call work or time limit.
The fresh six-second 6x CPU stall case passes. Both peers move 9.626 m
after CPU clear. By the first neutral check requested two seconds after
clear, both runners report live with backlog 0. This is sampled evidence,
not exact latency or a worst-case guarantee.

The missing-target refusal/rotation branch still needs a focused real
protocol test. Do not count an ordinary successful aged-room transfer as
coverage of a donor that lacks the exact requested checkpoint.

6. Verify deployment of the hosted intentional-leave repair.

The new client close signal and local dev kick exist. Sending is best
effort, without acknowledgement or retry. Older authorities or failed sends
can still use reconnect grace. Our Game SDK 5.28.0 has the required local
send/kick methods.

The rc.59 release commit includes production close-to-kick handling in
server/mp-room-server/src/rooms/SyncplayAuthorityGameRoom.ts:721-724.
The current local checkout has an older wrapper and is not the release
source. The implementation is present; identify the deployed server version
and verify that hosted rooms use it. Source inspection does not establish
the currently deployed server state.
Local aged-room departure/rejoin now takes 151/1204 ms. A focused normal
lifecycle case measures 336/565 ms. Local prompt removal is confirmed.
Keep those regressions. Verify hosted explicit leave, repeated close, and
unexpected socket loss retaining grace. Keep seat reservation policy intact.
