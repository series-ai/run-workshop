What changed in this pass
  1. Camera. The old obstacle search could switch viewing angles after smoothing. Animated limbs changed its result. The new camera preserves direction, uses one smooth state, and opens a local view through blocking structure. Resize and prop changes retain the selected angle.
  2. Figure construction. The shoulder and hip bars were a poor fit for the reference style. Arms and legs now share branch points on one spine. Body bands are removed. Proportions and equipment distinguish the characters.
  3. Style and motion. Strong action shapes, space between limbs, planted feet, and uneven timing create the energy in the references. This pass corrects those areas. Short effects support the body motion.
  4. Pale figures. A continuous skinned contour replaces the old surface-angle band. The edge width follows screen size.
  5. Hits and falls. Reactions follow the incoming force. A rear hit produces a forward fall. The target holds its landing position and uses the matching recovery clip.
  6. Industrial surfaces. Orange caps, strips, and collars have source geometry clearance. The correction also repairs several collar axes.

The remaining style gap is the drawn line itself. These 3D limbs still form straighter segments than the curved strokes in the Flash references. Front views can merge limbs, and the shared rig gives less individual performance than a hand-drawn film. Several secondary clips retain their prior poses.
